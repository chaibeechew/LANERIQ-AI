# LANERIQ AI Intellectual Property Notice & Takedown Procedure

**Version:** LANERIQ-IP-NOTICE-TAKEDOWN-v1.1-DRAFT  
**Corpus:** LANERIQ-LEGAL-CORPUS-2026.09.05-r1  
**Status:** DRAFT — QUALIFIED MALAYSIAN LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED BEFORE PUBLIC ACTIVATION  
**Last Harmonized:** 2026-09-05

This procedure governs the intended handling of copyright and other intellectual-property complaints relating to content, projects, listings or files hosted or made accessible through LANERIQ AI.

## 1. Purpose and scope

LANERIQ may provide hosting, build, preview, publishing, marketplace or storage functionality through which user-supplied or generated material becomes accessible.

This procedure is designed to preserve evidence, respond proportionately to credible infringement notices, protect legitimate users from abusive complaints and support any applicable Malaysian service-provider protections.

LANERIQ does not become the final legal decision-maker on ownership merely by applying a temporary restriction. Courts, tribunals and rights holders retain their legal roles.

## 2. Public designated contact / safe-harbour truth boundary

Before LANERIQ relies on any statutory service-provider protection that requires a designated complaint contact or prescribed information, Production activation must publish legally sufficient contact information and implement the required process.

The public record must not expose a residential address, private identity documents or other unnecessary private data merely to imitate compliance.

If legally sufficient business/service contact details are not yet available, the relevant safe-harbour claim remains **NOT READY** rather than publishing fabricated or private information.

## 3. Notice information

A claimant should provide enough information for a good-faith assessment, including as applicable:

- claimant name/contact details;
- identification of the protected work;
- identification/location of the allegedly infringing material;
- explanation of ownership/authority;
- a good-faith statement of unauthorized use where applicable;
- a statement that supplied information is accurate to the claimant's knowledge; and
- an electronic or other legally sufficient signature/affirmation where required.

LANERIQ may request missing information before treating a notice as complete.

## 4. Initial response and evidence preservation

Upon receiving a facially credible notice, LANERIQ may:

1. preserve the notice and relevant audit evidence;
2. restrict public access to specifically identified material where reasonably necessary;
3. notify the affected user unless law, safety or a legitimate investigation requires otherwise;
4. request additional evidence from either side; and
5. avoid deleting unrelated project content merely because one item is disputed.

Emergency restrictions may be used for clear infringement risk, court/regulatory orders, account takeover, malware, leaked credentials or another material legal/security threat.

## 5. Counter-notice / restoration

Where applicable law permits restoration after a counter-notice, settlement, court decision or other sufficient evidence, LANERIQ should maintain a documented challenge/restoration process.

Restoration must not occur solely because a user clicks a generic appeal button. The evidence record should preserve the notice, counter-notice, timestamps, relevant evidence and final action.

## 6. Repeat and abusive conduct

LANERIQ may suspend or restrict accounts that repeatedly infringe rights, knowingly submit false ownership claims, abuse the complaint process, sell projects they do not own or repeatedly evade prior restrictions.

A repeat-infringer policy must be applied proportionately and must not treat every disputed allegation as proven infringement.

## 7. Marketplace and Buyout holds

An active credible IP complaint, material ownership dispute or court/regulatory hold may block:

- App Sale completion;
- IP/source-code handover;
- ownership transfer;
- Buyout issuance where entitlement/ownership is materially disputed; or
- marketplace payout/release where the platform is authorized to control the relevant workflow state.

A hold preserves evidence/risk controls and is not a final adjudication of ownership.

## 8. Privacy and evidence minimization

Complaint records may contain personal data and confidential business information. Access must be limited to persons who need it for legal, safety, support or dispute handling.

Private keys, passwords, raw OTPs, complete identity documents and unnecessary personal data must not be included in public complaint records.

## 9. No admission

Removing, restricting, restoring or preserving material under this procedure is not an admission by LANERIQ that infringement did or did not occur.

## 10. Zero-new-cost current-stage implementation

At the current stage, LANERIQ may operate this procedure using existing authenticated accounts, support channels, audit logs and manual review without purchasing specialist takedown software.

No paid workflow provider is required merely to keep a legally reviewed process available. If law, volume or risk later requires specialist tooling, that change must be separately approved.

## 11. Relationship to App Sale/IP Assignment

A Seller's execution of an App Sale/IP Assignment Agreement does not cure a lack of authority to transfer the assets. The transfer workflow may remain blocked while a material ownership complaint is unresolved.

The App Sale agreement and Asset Schedule must preserve the distinction between Seller-owned transferred assets, third-party/open-source rights and LANERIQ reserved technology.

## 12. Legal review gate

Before public Production activation, qualified Malaysian counsel should confirm the final notice fields, service-provider/designated-contact requirements, timelines, restoration/counter-notice mechanics, repeat-infringer process and interaction with sections 43G/43H and other applicable provisions of the Copyright Act 1987.
